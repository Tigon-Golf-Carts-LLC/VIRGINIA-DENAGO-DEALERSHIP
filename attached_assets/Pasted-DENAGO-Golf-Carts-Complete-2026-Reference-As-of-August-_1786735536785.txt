DENAGO Golf Carts: Complete 2026 Reference

As of August 14, 2026, DENAGO is a rapidly growing, feature-heavy electric golf cart and low-speed vehicle brand. Its strongest selling points are lithium power, modern technology, aggressive pricing, comfortable independent suspension, four-wheel disc brakes, and factory-ready LSV equipment.

The honest verdict: DENAGO offers excellent equipment for the money, but it is a younger brand than Club Car, E-Z-GO, or Yamaha. The strength of the servicing dealer, parts inventory, pre-delivery inspection, and written warranty matters enormously.

Company background
DENAGO EV entered the American golf cart and neighborhood-electric-vehicle market around 2023.
DENAGO is backed by or associated with TAO Motor and Zhejiang Taotao Vehicles, an established powersports manufacturer. It is not a Kandi brand. Company profile and ownership background

The company purchased the former Dallas Morning News printing plant in Plano for approximately $43.5 million as a major American assembly and manufacturing facility. Plano factory acquisition

LiFePO₄ battery advantages

LiFePO₄ means lithium iron phosphate. Compared with traditional lead-acid batteries, it offers:

No battery watering
Lower weight
More consistent voltage under load
Faster charging
Longer expected cycle life
Less performance drop as the battery discharges
Integrated battery-management-system protection

The battery is still a high-voltage component. Owners should use the matching onboard charger and avoid opening the pack, installing incompatible batteries, connecting accessories directly to battery terminals or charging outside the manufacturer’s temperature limits.

A properly loaded Rover XL6 will not normally travel as far as an unloaded Nomad XL. DENAGO’s newer 32-mile loaded figure for XL6 and XXL is more useful than an unloaded best-case claim.

DENAGO’s current website and older dealer materials do not always agree.

CITY range: Current official page says 40+ miles. Many dealer sheets continue to advertise up to 80 miles. Do not use 80 miles as an unconditional claim without a current DENAGO test sheet.
CITY seating: Current official information says six passengers. Some earlier launch materials described five seats.
OXEN seating: DENAGO’s official page displays both “2” and “2+2.” The commercial dump-bed configuration is generally a two-passenger vehicle.
OXEN range: Official claim is 70+ miles, while current dealer listings often say approximately 50 miles under load.
Rover XXL seating: Current official figure is six passengers. Some online pages incorrectly describe it as eight passengers.
Scout 4: Sold by dealers and included in the federal recall, but missing from the current official product grid.
Warranty language: Current model pages advertise eight years on the lithium battery, while the available V3 written warranty contains an older contradictory reference to five years.

For TIGON’s website, listings should be based on the VIN, manufacturer’s certificate of origin and current build sheet, not copied from an older generic brochure.

Golf cart versus street-legal LSV

DENAGO markets many vehicles as both golf carts and Low-Speed Vehicles.

Under federal law, an LSV is a four-wheeled vehicle that:

Travels more than 20 MPH but no more than 25 MPH
Has a GVWR below 3,000 pounds
Meets the applicable federal LSV equipment requirements

Those requirements include lighting, turn signals, mirrors, windshield, reflectors, parking brake, seat belts, VIN and certification labeling. Federal LSV requirements

Street legality is not automatic everywhere. Registration, insurance, inspections, licensing and approved roads are controlled by state and local law.

Important points:

Lights and seat belts alone do not make a cart street legal.
Confirm the 17-digit VIN and federal certification label.
Confirm that the MCO identifies the correct vehicle class.
Many jurisdictions limit LSVs to roads posted at 35 MPH or below, but rules vary.
A 25 MPH LSV is not built to the same crashworthiness standards as a passenger car.
Increasing the maximum speed above 25 MPH can remove the vehicle from the federal LSV classification and may void the warranty.

“Medium Speed Vehicle” is not the correct federal classification for a standard 25 MPH DENAGO. For compliance language, use Low-Speed Vehicle or LSV unless a particular state separately recognizes an MSV category.

Current federal safety recall

DENAGO currently has an important NHTSA recall, Campaign 26V381, affecting approximately 34,136 vehicles.

Affected models include certain:

2024–2025 Nomad
2024–2026 Nomad XL
2026 Scout 2
2026 Scout 4
2024–2026 Rover XL
2024–2026 Rover XL6
2024–2026 Rover XXL

The issue is that affected vehicles may not produce the pedestrian-warning sound required under FMVSS 141. Because electric vehicles are quiet, pedestrians may not hear them moving.

The remedy is a free Acoustic Vehicle Alerting System kit. The recall does not carry a “Do Not Drive” or “Park Outside” warning. CITY and OXEN were not named in the published campaign summary. Recall 26V381 summary

Every owner and dealer should check the complete 17-character VIN using the official NHTSA recall lookup. DENAGO recall customer service is listed as 1-877-755-2453.

Recommended maintenance
Keep the battery charged according to the owner’s manual.
Never use an incompatible charger.
Do not connect high-draw accessories directly to the lithium pack.
Check tire pressure and wear regularly.
Check wheel-lug torque after initial use and at service intervals.
Inspect brake operation, fluid and parking brake.
Test lights, turn signals, horn, camera and pedestrian-warning system.
Inspect suspension, steering and alignment.
Keep battery, controller, charger and electrical connectors dry.
Avoid high-pressure washing around electrical equipment.
Check for loose wiring or connectors.
Use VIN-specific replacement parts.
Schedule professional inspections for heavily used rental or commercial carts.
Follow DENAGO instructions for long-term or winter storage.
Never modify maximum speed without understanding warranty and LSV consequences.
